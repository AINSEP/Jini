# Charts and exhibits

An exhibit is the proof for its slide's title. Write the title first, then pick the form.

## Pick the form from the question

| The title says… | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| A is bigger than B (compare items) | Horizontal bars, sorted | Pie, 3D, radar |
| It went up or down (change over time) | Line for many points; columns for a few periods | Area stacks with many series |
| X is most of the total (share) | One 100% bar, or a pie with ≤ 3 slices and one highlighted | Donut with many slices |
| Values spread out or cluster (distribution) | Histogram, box plot, dot strip | An average alone |
| X moves with Y (relationship) | Scatter, with a trend line if useful | Two lines on two y-axes |
| We got from A to B because… (bridge) | Waterfall | Stacked columns |
| Who leads (ranking) | Sorted bars or dot plot | Unsorted bars |
| Where it happens (place) | Map, only if location matters; otherwise bars | Choropleth of raw counts |
| One number matters | Big number with its comparison | Gauge, speedometer |
| When things happen (plan) | Timeline or Gantt | Bulleted dates |

Use several aligned charts on one slide when they support the same takeaway. Different takeaways
get different slides.

## Finish the chart

Make the chart say the title, in this order. Steps 2, 4 and 5 are defaults, not laws:

1. **Strip.** Remove borders, background fills, 3D, shadows and heavy gridlines. Keep light
   gridlines only if people need to read values.
2. **Mute.** Draw supporting data in a neutral grey. When series are categories people must tell
   apart (products, regions), keep their colours from the deck's colour key instead.
3. **Highlight.** Put the one bar, line or point that proves the title in the accent colour.
4. **Label directly.** Name lines at their ends and put values on the bars that matter, then drop
   the legend. If direct labels would collide, keep a compact legend. Drop the value axis if
   every bar is labelled.
5. **Add a reference** only when it means something here: target, average, last year or plan, as
   a thin dashed labelled line.
6. **Annotate.** One short callout where it matters ("Launch, Mar"), with a thin leader line if
   needed. Two at most.
7. **Round.** 2–3 significant figures. Units once, in the axis title or label. Same units across
   the deck.
8. **Source.** A small line at the bottom left: "Source: <who>, <date>".

## Axes and honesty

- Ordinary bars and columns that show magnitude start at zero. Lines may zoom in if the axis is
  clearly labelled.
- Waterfalls: the start and end bars rise from zero; each change bar floats from the previous
  running total. Label the start, every change and the end.
- Charts shown side by side share a scale.
- No dual y-axes. Use two aligned charts instead.
- Time runs left to right.
- Rank bars largest to smallest, top to bottom. Leave built-in sequences as they are: periods,
  age bands, rating scales from "disagree" to "agree".
- Show uncertainty when it changes the decision: a range, error bars, "est.".
- Don't cherry-pick the time window. If you crop it, say why in the notes.

## Tables

Use a table when people need exact values to look up.

- Presented: at most about 6 rows and 5 columns. Bigger tables go to the appendix.
- Right-align numbers and use the same decimals in a column.
- Thin row separators only; no full grid.
- Mark the row or cell that matters with weight or the accent.
- Header row and first column in plain words, with units.

## Diagrams

- **Process:** 3–6 steps left to right. Use arrows only for a real sequence.
- **2×2:** two axes that really are independent, each end labelled (low/high). Place items as
  dots. Name the quadrants only if that helps.
- **Breakdown tree:** whole on the left or top, parts after it. Parts don't overlap and add up to
  the whole.
- **Option comparison:** 2–4 options as columns, the same criteria as rows. Mark the chosen
  option with the accent.
- **Layers or pyramid:** only for a real hierarchy or stack.
- **Overlap circles:** only for real overlap, with the shared part labelled.

Rules for all diagrams: same shape for the same kind of thing, one line weight, text inside
shapes at body size or larger, no decorative arrows or 3D.

## Numbers on slides

- A big-number slide: one figure, 160–240 px at 1920×1080, with what it measures and its
  comparison ("+18% vs. plan").
- Format the same way everywhere: 1.2M, 34%, $4.5k.
- Don't rely on green and red to mean good and bad. Say "better" or "worse", or use arrows.
- Check every total adds up and every number matches across slides.

## Screenshots and photos as evidence

- Crop to the part that matters, then enlarge it.
- Point at the detail: an outline box, or dim everything else.
- Screenshots must be current and readable at slide size. Blur personal or secret data.

## Icons

- One family, one style (line or filled), one colour (neutral or the accent).
- Use an icon only when it adds meaning. Not one on every bullet.
- Prefer the literal icon to the clever one, and still label it.
